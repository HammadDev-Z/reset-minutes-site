Two cleaning product combinations come up again and again in official safety guidance. This guide shows exactly which ones Poison Control, the CDC and the EPA name, puts a source beside every line, and says plainly what those sources do **not** cover. It is general information, not medical advice.

::: safety
**If you think you have been exposed:** leave the area and get fresh air. If you have trouble breathing or other serious symptoms, seek medical care. In the US you can call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 ([Poison Control: chlorine gas](https://www.poison.org/articles/chlorine-gas)).
:::

## The two combinations official sources name {#the-two-combinations}

| Do not combine | What the source says it makes | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Bleach and ammonia** | Chloramine gas. Poison Control says it is less volatile than chlorine gas and so less likely to cause serious toxicity. The CDC still says to never mix bleach with ammonia. | [Poison Control: chlorine gas](https://www.poison.org/articles/chlorine-gas); [Poison Control: spring cleaning](https://www.poison.org/articles/spring-cleaning); [CDC: bleach](https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/safety/how-to-safely-clean-and-sanitize-with-bleach.html) |
| **Bleach and an acid**, such as vinegar or some toilet bowl cleaners | Chlorine gas. | [Poison Control: chlorine gas](https://www.poison.org/articles/chlorine-gas); [Poison Control: vinegar](https://www.poison.org/articles/vinegar-164); [Poison Control: spring cleaning](https://www.poison.org/articles/spring-cleaning) |

A 2020 CDC report on household cleaning names the same two pairs, bleach with vinegar and bleach with ammonia, and says heating these mixtures can also generate the gases ([CDC MMWR, 12 Jun 2020](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/69/wr/mm6923e2.htm)).

### Why the vinegar one catches people out

In that report's May 2020 survey of 502 US adults, only 35% said bleach should not be mixed with vinegar, while 58% said it should not be mixed with ammonia ([CDC MMWR](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/69/wr/mm6923e2.htm)). Vinegar feels harmless, but Poison Control's vinegar page says plainly not to mix it with chlorine bleach ([Poison Control: vinegar](https://www.poison.org/articles/vinegar-164)).

## The rule that covers everything else {#the-general-rule}

The pages we reviewed name only the two combinations above. What they say about the rest is a general rule, and it is the same from every source:

- **CDC:** never mix bleach with ammonia or any other cleaner ([CDC: bleach](https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/safety/how-to-safely-clean-and-sanitize-with-bleach.html), reviewed 26 Aug 2026).
- **Poison Control:** mixing cleaning products can cause poisonous fumes and serious breathing problems ([spring cleaning](https://www.poison.org/articles/spring-cleaning)). Its dish soap page says not to mix dish soap with other household products either ([Poison Control: dish soap](https://www.poison.org/articles/hand-dish-soap)).
- **CDC report:** it recommends avoiding mixing chemical products ([CDC MMWR](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/69/wr/mm6923e2.htm)).

So the practical rule is short: **use one product at a time.**

## What the sources do not tell you {#what-the-sources-do-not-say}

This part matters, because a chart can look more complete than it is.

- **There is no complete list.** The pages we reviewed name two combinations. We did not find an official page in our check that names other pairs, so we do not list any and we do not guess.
- **No wait time.** None of these pages says how long to wait, or how much to rinse, between one product and the next. We do not give a number. Read each product's label.
- **No "safe" pairs.** We do not say any combination is safe. The sources give a general rule, not a whitelist.

## Habits that follow from the sources {#habits}

- **Read the label first.** The EPA says to read the label before you buy, store and use household cleaners ([EPA: Read the label first](https://www.epa.gov/pets/read-label-first-protect-your-pets), updated 23 Mar 2026).
- **Keep products in their original containers.** The EPA says putting products in food or drink containers is dangerous. Poison Control's dish soap page also advises keeping products in original labeled containers ([EPA](https://www.epa.gov/pets/read-label-first-protect-your-pets); [Poison Control](https://www.poison.org/articles/hand-dish-soap)).
- **Ventilate.** Open a window or door, and run an exhaust fan, especially in small spaces such as bathrooms ([Poison Control: spring cleaning](https://www.poison.org/articles/spring-cleaning)). The CDC also says to open windows and doors when working indoors with bleach.
- **Protect yourself when using bleach.** The CDC recommends non-porous gloves and eye protection ([CDC: bleach](https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/safety/how-to-safely-clean-and-sanitize-with-bleach.html)).
- **Store cleaners out of reach.** Poison Control advises cabinets with child-resistant latches, and the EPA says to store cleaners where pets and children cannot reach them ([Poison Control](https://www.poison.org/articles/spring-cleaning); [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/pets/read-label-first-protect-your-pets)).

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use bleach and vinegar in the same cleaning session?**
Never mix them. The sources we reviewed do not say how long to wait or how much to rinse between products, so we cannot give a number. Use one product at a time and read each label.

**If bleach and ammonia make a less volatile gas, is mixing them less risky?**
Do not mix them. Poison Control says chloramine is less likely to cause serious toxicity than chlorine gas, but the CDC still says to never mix bleach with ammonia.

**Is dish soap safe to mix with other cleaners?**
Poison Control's dish soap page says not to mix dish soap with other household products, because it can produce dangerous fumes.

**Is there an official list of every bad combination?**
Not in the pages we reviewed. They name two combinations and give a general rule, which is why this guide ends with the rule instead of a longer chart.

**What should I do if I think I breathed fumes?**
Leave the area, get fresh air, and seek medical care if you have trouble breathing or other serious symptoms. In the US, Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222.
